## Notes on data and specifications in the paper. 
The replication package for this paper includes the data but not replication files with code; here I describe the specifications as they are described in the paper. I should get very close to their results; their dataset is cleaned and has few columns.

### Data
 The data cover the top 35 economics and psychology departments in the US and include:
- names (surname order is numerical A:Z --> 1:26)
- tenure status (at 3 tiers: untenured, tenured and emeritus)
- nationality, race, religion (to use as controls related to surname initial prevalence)
- whether they are fellows of the Econometric Society
- year of PhD completion (so time in field can be measured using 2006 as the year of observation)

### Outcomes
Tenure status is the outcome of focus for the two figures and two tables I will replicate.

## Table 1
Table 1 contains basic linear probability regressions of binary tenure outcomes on last name alphabetical index. Regressions are run restricting to different rank sets of departments (top five, top ten, top 20 and top 35—there); each set includes two regressions, the first of which includes no controls, and the second of which includes all controls mentioned above: (American) nationality, origin of the name (Jewish, Indian and other Asian). 

The authors report "experimenting" with additional control variables, including department fixed effects and cumulative number of publications, as well as with a sample restriction over year of receiving PhD.
